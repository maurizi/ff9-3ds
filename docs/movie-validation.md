# Movie display validation

## Implemented: packed RGB24 scanout (2026-10-16)

The original movie overlay selects `DISPENV.isrgb24` for full-screen movies.
Previously both DS scanout and PC frame dumps interpreted every framebuffer as
RGB555. They now read the selected format from `psx_gpu_display_info()`.

RGB24 uses three contiguous bytes per visible pixel, with display X expressed
in VRAM halfwords, not pixels. The PSX
[framebuffer format specification](https://psx-spx.consoledev.net/graphicsprocessingunitgpu/#framebuffer)
places red in bits 0–7, green in 8–15 and blue in 16–23. The display-start
register ignores X bit 0, also observed in the DuckStation experiment below.
The DS converts each sampled RGB8 triplet to its RGB555 bitmap with opacity
set, using the existing 4/5 nearest scene sampling. Packed movie frames bypass
the text-region coverage filter. There is no new framebuffer allocation.
Full-VRAM diagnostic dumps intentionally remain raw RGB555 interpretations;
only displayed-frame dumps use the selected RGB24 format.

## Implemented: bounded STR/MBG frame assembly

`port/psx/movie_stream.c` now assembles FF9's ten-sector frames: two 2048-byte
audio/metadata sectors, then eight 2324-byte video sectors with 32-byte headers
and reverse-ordered 2292-byte payloads. This follows the original overlay's
`func_8019BA70`, `func_8019BCE8`, `func_8019C434` and `func_8019C2F4`, and agrees
with the [FF9 STR format documentation](https://psx-spx.consoledev.net/dev/ps1/cdr/cdromfileformats/streaming/).
The assembler checks sector order, frame identity, dimensions, payload lengths
and duplicated BS headers. Errors latch until reset, and reset permits seeking
directly to a complete frame. It retains both camera records, control flags,
video headers and the complete padded video tail containing MBG extras.

The separate audio view validates AKAO lengths and block alignment and preserves
the original SPU pitch value. It identifies no-audio sectors and the AB-filled
terminal payload in disc-4 MBG116, rather than interpreting that marker as a
2.8-billion-byte sound block. ADPCM samples are not decoded or played yet.

`tools/movie_catalog.py` walks ISO9660 movie extents and runs the production C
assembler against every sector. Results from the user's four NTSC-U discs:

| disc | movie files | playable frames | frames with camera metadata | unused tail sectors |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 19 | 14,141 | 745 | 10,320 |
| 2 | 14 | 8,066 | 864 | 18,850 |
| 3 | 14 | 8,404 | 493 | 16,240 |
| 4 | 10 | 12,768 | 1,861 | 0 |
| total | 57 | 43,379 | 3,963 | 45,410 |

All playable frames assembled successfully, with 320×224 dimensions, BS version
2, and continuous frame numbers from 1 through the declared end. Of those,
42,033 frames contain AKAO audio, one has terminal sound padding, and the rest
are silent. The probe validates unused ISO tail sectors separately as frame-zero
padding; it does not feed them to the assembler as playable data.

The maximum compressed BS length observed is 18,336 bytes. The largest declared
expanded MDEC run-level size is 15,104 words (60,416 bytes, before its command
word). One assembler object takes 22,708 bytes on the host: it stores compressed
video, both full audio/metadata sectors, all eight video headers and bookkeeping.
It allocates nothing internally and has no global frame buffer. Future playback
must include decoder scratch space, sound queues and peak loading overlap in its
DS budget; this object is not the entire movie working set.

These checks establish stream framing and bounds, not decoded pixel or audio
fidelity; actual PSX movie captures remain necessary for decoder validation.

## Implemented: BS v2 coefficient expansion

`port/psx/movie_vlc.c` expands the compressed stream into the MDEC command and
run-level halfwords. Prefix groups, sign handling, escapes, DC quantization
bits and FE00 padding follow the original `func_8019AEC0` / `func_8019B00C`.
The caller supplies the user's 108 run/level pairs from `D_8019DCFC`; the
decoder neither embeds a new game-data table nor allocates a lookup buffer.
Input reads and output writes are bounded, and malformed or unsupported
streams return errors. Its output buffer can require up to 65,540 bytes under
the original 0x4000-word limit; this is additional to the compressed-frame
storage above. It performs no inverse quantization, IDCT or RGB conversion.

Verification on 2026-10-16:

* Native expansion succeeded for all **43,379 playable frames**, each producing
  the expected 1,680 blocks. Full-scan catalogs are under ignored
  `build/validation/movie_vlc_all_disc1.json` through `movie_vlc_all_disc4.json`.
* **171 frames** (beginning, middle and end of every movie) were expanded by
  FF9's original MIPS routine in PCSX-Redux, then compared byte-for-byte with
  native output, including command words and padding. All matched; the four
  bytes after each original output retained their canary value. Reports and
  paired compressed/reference/native streams are in
  `build/validation/movie_vlc_psx_disc1_ready` and `movie_vlc_psx_disc234`.
* A separate ARM9 test ROM linked with **`ds_arm9.specs`** ran in melonDS 1.1
  **original DS mode (67 MHz)**. Every synthetic prefix/sign, escape and bounds
  test passed, as did four PSX coefficient fixtures selected from the latter
  report (including its largest output and most compressed bits consumed).
  `movie_vlc_ds_done=1` and `movie_vlc_ds_failures=0` were read from the matching
  savestate. Evidence: `build/validation/movie_vlc_ds.png`, `.ml8`, and
  `movie_vlc_ds_result.json`; fixture identities are in

## Implemented: integer MDEC macroblock pixels

`port/psx/mdec_decode.c` now reconstructs one six-block Cr/Cb/Y macroblock at a
time, producing either packed RGB24 or RGB555 bytes. It accepts the caller's
uploaded quantization and scale tables, preserves signed/STP command flags,
handles leading FE00 padding and implicit block completion at coefficient 63,
and checks input/output bounds. It allocates no heap memory and needs no global
decoder buffer. The two matrix passes use integer sums and explicit floor/round
operations; there is no floating-point conversion or offline recompression.

The fixed-point precision and colour arithmetic are adapted from Mednafen's
GPL-2.0-or-later PSX MDEC research, with attribution, source revision and licence
in `third_party/mednafen/`. Scalar matrix passes retain the raw signed uploaded
scale values. This is a reconstruction API, not an emulated MDEC MMIO/DMA state
machine. It supports the two coloured output depths; monochrome output is not
implemented by this API.

Verification on 2026-10-16 used DuckStation `0.1-12080-g9cb25f7e9`, interpreter
mode and the user's original FF9 tables. The reference executes real MDEC
commands and collects the output through DMA1 into a guarded RAM buffer:

* **804 synthetic macroblocks** match byte-for-byte. These cover every AC rank
  in all six blocks with different signs, q scales and saturation levels,
  neutral/DC quadrant controls, both colour depths and signed/STP combinations.
* **2,688 real movie macroblocks** also match. The suite samples beginning,
  middle and end macroblocks from each of the 171 coefficient reference frames
  across all 57 movie files, and expands the sampling to every macroblock of
  three selected frames in both output depths.
* The three reconstructed **320×224 RGB24 frames** match pixel-for-pixel:
  disc-2 FMV031 frame 1 (detailed ocean view), disc-2 MBG110 frame 195 (black end
  frame), and disc-3 MBG112 frame 60 (detailed pool/room). The paired PNGs are
  assembled from macroblock DMA captures using the original player's column
  order; they are not screenshots of composed field gameplay or a playing movie.
* The separate test ROM, linked with `ds_arm9.specs`, passed the unit suite and
  **five captured PSX pixel fixtures in original DS mode at 67 MHz** in melonDS
  1.1. The matching saved state has `mdec_ds_done=1`, `mdec_ds_failures=0`.
  This checks ARM9 execution, not original DS hardware or full-game DS support.

The impulse comparison caught an integer IDCT pass-order error: vertical must
precede horizontal, because rounding makes the otherwise commuting transforms
different. The signed RGB555 comparison caught rounding at the byte-127
boundary. Both have independent PSX golden assertions in the host/ARM9 unit
tests. A synthetic q=63/DC=-512 case was removed as invalid test input because
its encoded value is FE00, reserved as padding; the valid saturation case uses
q=62. Failed diagnostic attempts remain under ignored `build/validation/` and
were not counted as passing evidence.

All 3,492 final comparisons and paired raw output files are in
`build/validation/mdec_psx_movies/report.json` and its directory. Full frames
are `frame_081_{psx,port}.png`, `frame_095_{psx,port}.png`, and
`frame_121_{psx,port}.png`; their identities are in `full_frames.json`.
ARM9 evidence is `build/validation/mdec_ds.png`, `.ml8`, and
`mdec_ds_result.json`; fixture identities are in `build/tests/mdec_fixture.json`.
Both emulators were closed and configurations restored after testing.

At `-O2 -mthumb` for ARM946E-S, GCC's stack-usage report gives 1,064 bytes for

a 512/768-byte output buffer. This avoids another full-frame allocation. These
are component memory measurements, not a demonstrated total movie budget or a
real-time performance result.

The comparison establishes agreement with this emulator on these inputs. It
does not prove exact original-hardware rounding for arbitrary custom tables,
all MDEC modes, or every movie pixel. The complete game, final compositing,
animation and audio fidelity requirements remain open.

## Implemented: the original player drives playback (2026-10-17)

The placeholder player is gone. The decomp's ovl_64 C (`MBG_init`, `mbg_play`,
`mbg_update`, the sector ring, camera/mask bookkeeping, pause and end
conditions) now runs unchanged. `port/game/movie.c` supplies only what is still
hand-written asm there:

* `mbg_vlcDecode` calls `ff9_movie_vlc_v2` with the original `g_mbgVlcPairs`,
  bounded by the frame's ring slot. `mbg_vlcBuildTable` has nothing to build.
* `mbg_applyMask`, translated from the original routine, including its
  do-while edges and the `FE n` column fill that compares with `n`.
* libpress `DecDCTReset/in/out/inSync/outSync/outCallback` over
  `psx_mdec_macroblock`, with the quantization/scale tables uploaded by the
  original reset (`D_8019DEC8`, `D_8019DF4C`). Decoding is synchronous; an out
  request made from the out callback is run by the outer call's loop, so the
  player's slice-by-slice `LoadImage` order is preserved without recursion.

Streamed sectors now arrive at drive speed: `psx_cd_frame` delivers 75 or 150
sectors per second (`CdlModeSpeed`) once per 60 Hz frame, while loader polls keep
their one-sector pumps. Movies read without polling, so they need the real rate:
ten sectors per 15 fps movie frame.

Observed on the PC build (software renderer frame dumps):

* The attract loop matches the PSX order: Squaresoft logo, intro FMV with the
  map overlays, the title menu over its animated background, the character
  motto cards, then the intro again.
* New Game plays field 70's opening FMV (the storm, Garnet, the Prima Vista,
  the staff credits) at 15 movie frames per four VSyncs, then loads field 50,
  the dark room. The `candle` scenario follows the same path.
* Movie audio through AKAO's streamed-sound commands: the intro's 44 seconds
  have no silent 20 ms window once the music starts.

2,688 real-movie macroblocks of the PSX comparison set: 395 us per macroblock

area is unchanged (`psx_vram_generation`), which covers three of every four
VSyncs during a 15 fps movie. The SPU is now the largest single cost here and
everywhere else in the game.

## FastVideoDS transcode: measured (2026-10-26)

STR decoding costs about 105 ms a VBlank on an original DS (MDEC 66, VLC 10), so full-screen movies are to be
transcoded at build time (design 6.3). FastVideoDS has no licence, so it stays an external checkout: `fastvideo`
is a gitignored symlink to the player, and `tools/docker.sh` mounts its target. The encoder (C#, .NET 8,
FFmpeg 5.1) runs from its own checkout. It needed local patches: no audio stream (the audio stays the game's own
XA), net8.0, and `docker run -t` (it reads the console width).

* `tools/movie_export.c` (host) decodes one movie extent of a raw disc image to 320x224 RGB24 frames. It uses the
  port's frame assembly, BS v2 expansion and MDEC, with a tables file holding the movie overlay's
  `g_mbgVlcPairs`, `D_8019DEC8` and `D_8019DF4C` (216+128+128 bytes, taken from a host RAM dump during a movie).
  FFmpeg scales those frames for the encoder.
* `make fmv-bench` builds `build/tests/fv_bench.nds` (decoder code and tables in main RAM, as the port would load
  them) and `fv_bench_itcm.nds` (code in ITCM). Both build only when the `fastvideo` checkout is present. They
  decode `fat:/testVideo.fv` frame by frame and show the decode time per frame.

melonDS, original DS (67 MHz), the intro (FMVD001, 740 frames, 15 fps), CPU time per movie frame:

| build | average | worst |
|---|---:|---:|
| decoder in main RAM, VRAM cached (MPU region 3) | 33 ms | 53 ms |
| decoder in ITCM | 27.6 ms | |

A 15 fps frame has 66 ms. Each P-frame also waits two VBlanks for the 3D engine's motion compensation and capture.

Height: a P-frame's residual is 192 bytes per 8x8 block, written from bank D on (LCD 0x06860000). At 256x192 it
is 144 KB, which overflows the 128 KB of bank D. The overflow is read back as zero, shows as black blocks, and
spreads up from the bottom through the motion compensation. Heights up to 170 fit in bank D; mapping bank E as
LCD as well (contiguous after D) makes 256x192 decode cleanly. The movies have square pixels (320x224 on a 4:3
screen), so they scale to 256x179. They are encoded as 256x176: three rows cropped (one at the top, two at the
bottom). Padding to 184 with black rows instead left the encoder's blocks ringing at the edge between the padding
and the picture.

## Implemented: transcoded movies on the DS (2026-10-26)

`tools/fmv_transcode.py --disc N` writes `build/fmv/dN/LBA.fv` for every STR on the disc (40 movies, 364 MB),
video only. `make fv-blob` builds `build/fv.bin` from the `fastvideo` checkout. It contains the decoder's code and
tables linked at 0, with their relocations (`tools/fv_blob.py`, `port/ds/fv/`). On the SD card the files go in
`ff9ds/fv.bin` and `ff9ds/fmv/dN/`. Without them, movies play through the MDEC as before. The PC build always
uses the MDEC.

* `port/game/movie.c`: when the player expands a frame with the movie in 24-bit mode and a transcode exists, it
  starts the transcode (`port/ds/movie_fv.c`). The frame's VLC is skipped, its MDEC slices complete without data
  (the player's callbacks still run, and `LoadImage` drops their uploads), and the STR frame number from the ring
  slot's sector header selects the `.fv` frame. Leaving 24-bit mode (a movie going on behind a field) hands back
  to the MDEC. `DecDCToutCallback(0)` (`mbg_stop`) stops it.
* The decoder is loaded into the player's two 64 KB VLC buffers. Banks A-C hold frames, D and E the residual and
  the display list. MPU region 2 makes LCD VRAM cacheable for the duration. The software GPU and the hybrid
  renderer rest: nothing drawn shows over a 24-bit movie, on the PS1 either. Afterwards the display is set up
  again at the next present.
* A bank can't be both the reference and on screen, so frame n shows once n+1 is decoded. The next frame's
  triangles go to the geometry engine while the current one is captured and finished, and its swap is written
  only once its reference is complete. The texture is read only when the engine renders, after the swap.
  Everything runs from the player's frame and between the game's own work (`movie_fv_idle` in `psx_frame` and
  `pace`). The game waits only when the frame to show isn't decoded at all.
* `mbg_streamGetFrame` is overridden (`config/overrides.txt`). A look for a frame not there yet is a `VSync(-1)`
  poll. The player looks 1000 times and then seeks the stream again, dropping the ring. On the PS1 the drive goes
  on delivering during those looks; here virtual time only moved between frames. A faster player therefore spun
  through its looks with the drive standing still, then seeked. This cost about 12 ms per VBlank, and the stream
  re-read.

melonDS, original DS, intro (FMVD001), ms per PS1 VBlank (`PROFILE=1`, 128-frame windows ending at frames 1758
and 2398):

| build | 1758 | 2398 |
|---|---:|---:|
| MDEC (2026-10-17, DSi mode) | ~81 | |
| transcode, first working | 20.9 | 32.5 |
| + no software GPU, no busy-wait for the swap | 20.3 | 32.4 |
| + stream looks as polls | 21.3 | 20.0 |
| + swap deferred, decoding between the game's work, 176 rows | 19.9 | 18.6 |
| + residual decoded while the engine swaps and captures; ADPCM word decoding | 16.7 | 16.65 |

The PS1 takes 16.7: the intro now plays in real time. A P-frame's residual decoding (`dcts`, 2.9 ms per VBlank)
needs neither the prediction nor the reference, so it runs as soon as the previous frame is finished; only adding
it to the captured prediction (`finish`) waits for the capture. At 2398: residual decoding 2.9 ms, the rest of the
decoder 4.9, SPU 3.5 (its ADPCM blocks now decoded a word of nibbles at a time; the unused noise generator no
longer ticks in hardware mode), disc sectors 1.0.

Frames show when the player shows them: a frame can't be shown while it is the next one's reference, so the player's
frame n needs n+1 decoded. The first frames build that one-frame lead (the player waits for it) and the decoding
keeps it (frame n+1 already decoded at 519 of 522 frames by 2398, 16.74 ms per VBlank). Keeping it needed the
chain from one frame's finish to the next's within four VBlanks: the capture is armed with the swap when the swap
is written mid-frame (it starts at the next line 0, after the swap), and the swap waits for its triangles' DMA
instead of retrying later.

## Movies in fields: survey (2026-10-26)

Fields start movies through event opcode 0x28 (`fld_movieCommand` 0: `fld_movieStart(list, entry, rgb24)`), which
names an entry of ovl_64's `g_mbgMovieLists` (5 lists, one per disc's movies plus the SPARE/TEST list). The flag
comes from the script, not the file. MBG files are shown full-screen as well as behind fields, so all 17 are
transcoded too (`fmv_transcode.py` takes `.MBG`: 4,832 frames). The player logs each movie's LBA and mode.

Entering every field fixture on the host (`build/validation/fieldsweep/cfg`, 3000 frames) starts ten movies:

| fixture | movie | mode |
|---|---|---|
| d1_f0061 | LBA 179678 | 24-bit |
| d1_f0070 | LBA 286280 (FMV001, the opening) | 24-bit |
| d1_f0100 | MBG101 | 24-bit |
| d1_f0209 | MBG102 | 24-bit |
| d2_f1001 | MBG106 (the Iifa Tree) | 24-bit, then the field |
| d2_f1464 | MBG108 (the forest before the Black Mage Village) | 15-bit, behind the field |
| d2_f1661 | MBG110 | 24-bit |
| d4_f2752 | FMV055A | 24-bit |
| d4_f2929 | MBG115 | 24-bit |
| d4_f2933 | FMV056 | 24-bit |

On the DS (original DS, transcodes staged) d2_f1001 plays MBG106 from its transcode and hands over to the field.
d2_f1464's 15-bit MBG still goes through the VLC and the MDEC: it shows correctly behind the characters, at 106 ms
per VBlank (VLC 11, MDEC 67; PS1 16.7).

The MBGs are full camera moves (a crane over Alexandria, the approach to the Iifa Tree), so they need a real video
codec, and FastVideoDS's 3D motion compensation can't share the 3D engine and VRAM with a field. Plan (the user's
choice of 2026-10-26: closest fidelity at full speed):

1. Motion compensation on the CPU, as the encoder models the 3D engine's prediction (its two blended passes and
   their rounding), checked against the 3D path on the full-screen movies.
2. 15-bit MBG frames from the transcode straight into the field's background bitmap (the area-scaled 256 wide
   frame in place of the nearest-scaled 320 wide one), skipping the VLC, the MDEC and the PS1 VRAM upload.
3. The mask (which background pixels cover the characters): the field draws its models with the GPU's mask
   check. Native field models don't apply it yet; to check against the PS1 on d2_f1464.
